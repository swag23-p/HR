01

The problem

Most HR teams find out someone is leaving in the exit interview — after the decision is already made. I wanted to know earlier, and put a number on what waiting costs.

60 employee records analyzed across six departments — Sales, Engineering, HR, Finance, Operations, Marketing
66.7% Engineering had the highest attrition rate of any department, more than double Sales

The dataset here is a sample built to mirror a real HRIS export — same structure a company's actual people data would take: tenure, overtime, manager ratings, engagement scores, time since last promotion or raise, commute distance. Point the model at real records and every number on this page recalculates.

The action plan

A model is only useful if it changes what HR does next. Each finding maps to an owner and a priority.

FindingActionOwnerPriority
Low engagement score is the single biggest predictor of attrition Quarterly pulse surveys, plus manager coaching on running better 1:1s HR Manager High
No promotion in 3+ years correlates strongly with high risk scores Launch a mid-career review and internal mobility program HRBP High
Employees on regular overtime show elevated attrition Review workload distribution in the affected departments Dept Heads Medium
No raise in 3+ years adds measurable risk Run an annual compensation benchmarking review against market Comp & Benefits Medium
Commutes over 25km are a smaller but real factor Explore hybrid or remote options for affected roles HR Manager Low
